WEST BROMWICH ALBION

LESS DETAIL.
MORE ALBION.

A proposal to preserve Albion’s most recognisable heritage — the Throstle, the Hawthorn, Navy & White stripes and the name ALBION — while refining how those assets perform across contemporary football culture.

PROPOSAL STATUS

This project is currently at proposal stage. Implementation has not been confirmed, and whether the project will proceed is currently under review.

01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

INDEPENDENT PROPOSAL

Heritage, refined.
Albion, redefined.

West Bromwich Albion already owns a distinctive visual language: the Throstle, the Hawthorn, Navy & White stripes and more than a century of club memory.

This proposal does not attempt to replace that heritage. It asks how the same story can become clearer, stronger and more flexible across today’s stadium, kit, merchandise and digital environments.

The ambition is therefore not reinvention for its own sake. It is to identify what is uniquely Albion, remove only the visual noise that limits recognition, and organise the remaining assets into a system that can work consistently across every club touchpoint.

HERITAGEKeep what makes Albion unmistakable.

Throstle, Hawthorn, stripes and the club name remain the foundation.

REFINEMENTReduce detail, not meaning.

Complexity is removed only where it improves recognition and consistency.

SYSTEMMove from one crest to one identity.

The crest, symbol, stripes and wordmark operate together across every touchpoint.

Preservation through Simplification.
Less Detail. More Albion.

02 / 1878 — THE BEGINNING

BORN IN WEST BROMWICH

Carried forward as Albion.

The club that became West Bromwich Albion was formed in West Bromwich in 1878. Its earliest pioneers were known as West Bromwich Strollers before the club took on the Albion name.

From that local beginning, the club grew with its community and built a football identity inseparable from place, supporters and matchday culture. The club’s history is therefore not treated as decorative reference material in this proposal; it is the design brief itself.

The central question is not how to reinvent Albion, but how a club born in West Bromwich can carry its history and symbols into the next generation while keeping Albion as its clearest shared identity.

PLACEWest Bromwich

The origin, local foundation and community context from which the club emerged.

IDENTITYAlbion

The name that came to represent the club, its team and generations of supporters.

CONTINUITY1878 → Today

A heritage to carry forward more clearly, not a past to be replaced.

The problem was never the heritage.
It was how the heritage was expressed.

03 / THE THROSTLE

A SYMBOL OF ALBION

Preserved through a clearer silhouette.

The Throstle — a Black Country term for the song thrush — has been associated with Albion since the nineteenth century and remains one of the club’s most direct identifiers.

Rather than turning the bird into a generic sports icon, the proposal keeps the recognisable upward posture, spotted chest, wing character and relationship with the branch while reducing unnecessary visual noise.

The bird is more than a mascot. It connects Club / Team / Supporters / Albion in one historic symbol. Detail is reduced, but the posture and silhouette remain recognisably Albion.

Less detail, stronger recognition.

04 / THE HAWTHORN

THE SYMBOL OF HOME

The club standing on its home.

The Hawthorns has been Albion’s home since 1900, and the hawthorn beneath the Throstle creates a rare relationship between emblem and place.

Removing it would make the mark simpler, but also less specific. The proposal therefore keeps the Hawthorn while reducing leaves, berries and small details so the idea remains legible at every scale.

If the Throstle represents the Club, the Hawthorn represents Home. Together, they complete the story of West Bromwich Albion.

THROSTLEClub

The historic bird becomes the clearest sign of Albion itself.

HAWTHORNHome

The tree links the identity directly to The Hawthorns and its place.

TOGETHERWest Bromwich Albion

Club and home remain one continuous story rather than separate graphic devices.

05 / NAVY & WHITE

MORE THAN COLOUR

A matchday memory.

Navy and white stripes are among Albion’s strongest visual assets.

Instead of adding a new palette to signal modernity, this proposal strengthens what supporters already recognise. The previous naturalistic brown, green and red details are removed from the core identity so the crest and secondary mark can operate as a unified Navy & White system.

The stripes are more than a graphic pattern. They are a memory of Home Kit / Match Day / Albion — an immediate visual rhythm that can carry recognition even when the full crest is not present.

ALBION NAVY

Tradition / Authority / Heritage

Primary identity, typography, stadium graphics and high-recognition applications. The proposed core navy is #091453.

WHITE

Clarity

Contrast, modernity and space. White gives the identity breathing room and allows the navy system to remain direct and highly legible.

06 / WHY REBRAND?

KEEP THE STORY

Change the way the story performs.

The existing crest contains meaningful history, but a highly detailed, multi-colour emblem becomes harder to control as the club moves between a shirt badge, mobile icon, broadcast graphic, social avatar, stadium sign and merchandise mark.

The opportunity is not to make Albion look less historic. It is to let that history remain recognisable in more environments and more production methods without asking every application to reinterpret the crest.

BEFORE / CREST-LED

Rich story, limited flexibility.

Multiple naturalistic colours · Detailed bird illustration · Complex Hawthorn construction · Detail loss at small sizes · One crest carrying every role.

AFTER / SYSTEM-LED

Same story, clearer hierarchy.

Navy / White core system · Clearer Throstle silhouette · Reduced Hawthorn detail · Improved small-scale recognition · Primary and secondary identities.

Heritage is not reduced.
Its expression becomes more controlled.

07 / REBRANDING FOCUS

PRESERVATION THROUGH SIMPLIFICATION

Keep the story.
Reduce the form.

Preservation through Simplification is the central strategy.

Simplification is not used to remove meaning. It is used to make meaning easier to recognise, reproduce and remember.

Form is reduced not simply to look contemporary, but to allow the meaning to travel more clearly across the modern football environment — from embroidery and signage to mobile interfaces, broadcast graphics and supporter merchandise.

01 / HERITAGE

Preserve

Retain the historic symbols and visual structures supporters already associate with Albion.

02 / SIMPLICITY

Reduce

Remove unnecessary colour and detail so the essential form can carry more recognition.

03 / RECOGNITION

Clarify

Create a silhouette that can be identified quickly and reliably, even at small scale.

04 / FLEXIBILITY

Expand

Build an identity that works consistently across kit, stadium, digital, social and merchandise.

08 / THE NEW CREST

CLEARLY NEW. UNMISTAKABLY ALBION.

The same story,
with a clearer hierarchy.

The proposal retains the shield, WEST BROMWICH / ALBION naming, Throstle, Hawthorn and Navy & White stripes.

The change happens in proportion, line weight, colour hierarchy and illustration detail. The goal is a crest that feels renewed without breaking the recognition built by the club’s history.

The ideal response is not “this is a completely different Albion,” but rather “this is clearly new, yet still unmistakably Albion.”

KEEPShield / Name / Throstle

The recognisable architecture of the club badge remains visible.

KEEPHawthorn / Stripes

Home and kit heritage remain embedded in the crest.

REFINEColour / Detail / Proportion

Fewer colours, cleaner illustration and stronger balance improve performance.

09 / THROSTLE MARK

A TWO-LEVEL IDENTITY SYSTEM

Crest represents the Club.
Throstle represents the Culture.

A football club no longer communicates through a crest alone.

The Throstle and Hawthorn are therefore separated into a secondary mark that can carry Albion’s identity in more informal, expressive and culture-led moments — without replacing the official crest.

The official Crest carries tradition and authority. The Throstle Mark works more freely across training wear, merchandise, social media, signage, flags and pattern. This allows the identity to behave like a contemporary club system rather than asking one detailed badge to perform every role.

PRIMARY CREST / TRADITION

Official Club Identity

Match kit · Stadium · Official communications · Competition · Formal club representation.

THROSTLE MARK / CULTURE

Contemporary Albion

Training wear · Merchandise · Social media · Signage · Flags · Patterns · Supporter culture.

Not simply a redesigned crest.
A renewed identity for Albion.

10 / APPLICATIONS

DESIGNED TO LIVE BEYOND THE BADGE

One visual language.
Many club environments.

The value of the system is tested through application.

Primary and secondary marks, Navy & White contrast, stripes and ALBION can shift in hierarchy depending on the environment while retaining one recognisable visual language.

The aim is consistency without monotony: enough structure to feel unmistakably Albion, but enough flexibility for matchday, retail, digital content, stadium spaces and supporter culture to each have their own energy.

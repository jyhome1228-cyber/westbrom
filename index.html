WEST BROMWICH ALBION

LESS DETAIL.
MORE ALBION.

A proposal to preserve Albion’s most recognisable heritage — the Throstle, Hawthorn, Navy & White and the name ALBION — while refining how those assets perform across contemporary football culture.

PROPOSAL STATUS

This project is currently at proposal stage. Implementation has not been confirmed, and whether the project will proceed is currently under review.

01 / THE STARTING POINT

BORN IN WEST BROMWICH

Carried forward as Albion.

The club that became West Bromwich Albion was formed in West Bromwich in 1878. Its earliest pioneers were known as West Bromwich Strollers before the club took on the Albion name.

From that local beginning, the club grew with its community and built an identity inseparable from place, supporters and matchday culture. This proposal therefore begins with a simple distinction: West Bromwich is the origin. Albion is the shared club identity carried forward.

The question is not how to reinvent Albion, but how to make its history and symbols clearer for the next generation without disconnecting them from the club supporters already know.

PLACEWest Bromwich

The origin and local foundation.

IDENTITYAlbion

CONTINUITY1878 → Today

A heritage to refine, not replace.

02 / THE HERITAGE

KEEP WHAT MAKES ALBION UNMISTAKABLE

The story is already strong.

The strongest opportunity is not to invent a new identity, but to give Albion’s existing assets a clearer hierarchy and a more flexible system.

01

The Throstle

The historic bird remains the clearest symbol of Albion itself — club, team and supporters in one recognisable icon.

02

The Hawthorn

The branch beneath the bird connects the identity directly to The Hawthorns and the idea of home.

03

Navy & White

More than colour, the striped visual language is a matchday memory and one of the club’s strongest recognition assets.

04

ALBION

Within the full club name, ALBION functions as the strongest verbal link between club culture and supporters.

The problem was never the heritage.
It was how the heritage was expressed.

03 / THE PROPOSAL

PRESERVATION THROUGH SIMPLIFICATION

Keep the story.
Reduce the form.

Simplification is not used here to remove meaning. It is used to make meaning easier to recognise, reproduce and remember.

The proposal retains the shield, WEST BROMWICH / ALBION naming, the Throstle, Hawthorn and Navy & White stripes. Change happens in proportion, line weight, colour hierarchy and illustration detail.

01 / HERITAGE

Preserve

Retain the symbols and visual memory supporters already recognise.

02 / SIMPLICITY

Reduce

Remove visual noise without removing the story behind the badge.

03 / RECOGNITION

Clarify

Strengthen the silhouette so the club is readable at any scale.

04 / FLEXIBILITY

Expand

Build a system that can work across kit, stadium, digital and merchandise.

04 / THE NEW CREST

CLEARLY NEW. UNMISTAKABLY ALBION.

The same story,
with a clearer hierarchy.

The goal is not to make Albion look less historic. It is to let that history remain recognisable across more environments.

05 / CREST + CULTURE

A TWO-LEVEL IDENTITY SYSTEM

Crest represents the Club.
Throstle represents the Culture.

A modern football club no longer communicates through a crest alone. The Throstle and Hawthorn therefore become a secondary mark that can operate more freely without replacing the official crest.

PRIMARY CREST / TRADITION

Official Club Identity

Match kit, official communications, competitions, formal club representation.

THROSTLE MARK / CULTURE

Contemporary Albion

Training wear, merchandise, signage, social media, flags and graphic patterns.

06 / APPLICATIONS

DESIGNED TO LIVE BEYOND THE BADGE

The system is tested through real-world application: on the pitch, at The Hawthorns, with supporters and across club culture.

07 / A PERSONAL NOTE

FROM SOUTH KOREA, WITH RESPECT FOR ALBION

This was made as a proposal,
not as a commission.

This project was created independently from South Korea, not as an official commission, but from respect for the history and identity of West Bromwich Albion.

The intention is not to claim ownership over the club’s heritage, but to explore how its existing symbols could be refined for contemporary football culture while remaining recognisably Albion.

If the Club finds any part of this direction meaningful, I would be happy to share the original design files and discuss the proposal further.
